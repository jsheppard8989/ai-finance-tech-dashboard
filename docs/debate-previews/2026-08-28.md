## Contract

Will the Federal Open Market Committee raise the federal funds target range at its
September 15-16, 2026 meeting?

- Starting range: 3.50% to 3.75%
- YES: The September statement raises both bounds of the target range.
- NO: The range is unchanged or reduced.
- Resolution source: The Federal Reserve's official September FOMC statement.

## Required context for both arguments

The reviewed evidence packet requires both sides to address:

1. Chair Kevin Warsh's August 28 Jackson Hole framework: inflation first, broad financial
   conditions not restrictive, no forward-guidance commitment, and “a discipline, not a decision.”
2. July PCE and CPI inflation against payrolls, unemployment, and labor-force participation,
   including the August jobs and CPI releases due before the decision.
3. The distinction between the overnight policy rate and 10-year and 30-year Treasury yields,
   including yen intervention, global capital flows, and Treasury's long-end buybacks.
4. The federal deficit, nondiscretionary outlays, net interest, total debt, and the verified
   $8.374 trillion twelve-month maturity burden—without treating Treasury financing cost as the
   Fed's mandate.
5. The midterms as a test of Fed independence, not evidence of politically motivated voting.
6. The strongest evidence against each speaker's own conclusion and why market odds are not proof.

## Factual correction

Scott Bessent's “whatever it takes” statement referred to supporting Japan's effort to stabilize
the yen. It was not a promise to cap long-term Treasury yields. Separately, Treasury doubled the
maximum size of planned long-end liquidity-support buybacks from $2 billion to at least $4 billion
per operation beginning September 9.
