## Contract

Will IGV, the iShares Expanded Tech-Software ETF, trail the SPY exchange-traded fund by at least
4 percentage points of total return from the close on 2026-08-28 to the close on 2026-10-09?

- Crux theme: AI vs the wage bill
- Resolves: Friday 2026-10-09 on official closing prices, total return
- YES if IGV total return minus SPY total return is ≤ −4.00 percentage points

## Affirmative — Ruby J. To Low

The long of it is that IGV, the iShares ETF focusing on tech-software sector, has been under
pressure due to the changing dynamics of enterprise software sales. Traditionally, tech companies
have sold software based on the number of human users, or seats. However, as Salesforce's
announcement on June 25, 2026, showed - with their pay-per-resolution pricing model for 'Agentforce
Help Agent', we see the beginning of a shift where software costs are tied to outcomes rather than
human activity. This move could potentially decouple software revenue from human headcount,
impacting IGV's companies.

According to the Stanford Digital Economy Lab's report on August 12, 2026, there is a significant
reduction in employment among young workers in highly AI-exposed sectors, suggesting that AI
adoption is leading to fewer hiring opportunities in these areas. Although the study doesn't
establish a direct causal link, it points towards a trend that could further impact the revenue
models of IGV's holdings.

Looking at historical performance, IGV significantly underperformed SPY in the past year, with a
23.96 point gap in total returns. This performance gap, coupled with the shift towards
outcome-based pricing and the potential impact of AI on wage bills, suggests that IGV could
continue to trail SPY.

Concession. While IGV has shown some signs of recovery recently, outperforming SPY over the past 29
trading sessions, this may not be sustainable given the structural changes occurring in the
tech-software sector. Key indicators to watch would include any reversal in the trend of employment
reduction in AI-exposed sectors or a shift back to traditional per-seat software pricing models.

## Negative — Chris Dixon

The short of it is that while AI is certainly changing the tech landscape, the proposition that IGV
will trail SPY by at least 4 percentage points in total return from late August to early October
2026 is not a foregone conclusion.

Firstly, IGV's composition is not solely dependent on AI-driven wage substitution. As of August 26,
2026, its largest holdings include companies like Palantir, Microsoft, and Oracle, which have
significant stakes in AI infrastructure and cybersecurity - areas where budgets do not necessarily
track headcount. This diversification could provide some insulation against wage-related risks.

Secondly, while the Stanford Digital Economy Lab's report does show lower employment in highly
AI-exposed occupations among young workers, it also notes that there is no widespread job
displacement economy-wide due to AI. This indicates that the impact of AI on the broader labor
market and, by extension, on IGV's performance may be more nuanced than suggested.

Thirdly, historical performance shows that the median absolute spread between IGV and SPY over the
past five years is 3.41 points, and IGV has outperformed SPY in the same late-August-to-early-October
window three out of the past five years. Seasonality and recent performance trends hint at a
potential for IGV to outperform or at least narrow the gap with SPY.

Concession. The most significant uncertainty revolves around the continued adoption of outcome-based
pricing models in enterprise software. If such models gain broad traction, it could indeed put
significant downward pressure on IGV's performance relative to SPY. Conversely, should this trend
reverse or prove less disruptive than anticipated, IGV may hold up better against SPY.
