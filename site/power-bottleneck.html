Who Owns the Energized Megawatt?

As of Nov 5, 2026, 2:35 PM CT

The short version

Our view (OPINION): we favor the companies that get paid on every new megawatt (MW; a gigawatt, GW, is 1,000 MW), with GE Vernova as the cleanest name. For the next 12 to 24 months, an AI chip is only worth as much as the energized megawatt under it.

The scarce pieces are a gas-turbine delivery slot for new builds and existing grid-connected plants that already hold capacity megawatts. Of those two, the turbine slot has the cleaner upside, because its price is not capped. Existing plants get paid, but PJM's capacity auction sits at a regulator-approved cap through the 2029/30 auction.

We test the view with a paper Jan 15, 2027 $860/$900 call debit spread on GE Vernova (GEV), held to expiry. Max loss is the $1,420 debit; max gain is $2,580.

How to read the labels. FACT comes from a primary source, linked where it applies. REPORTED comes from a named secondary source. INFERENCE is our own arithmetic. OPINION is our judgment.

Our view

OPINION Chips without power, generic developers, and projects sitting in the interconnection queue are the plentiful, low-pricing-power side. The junction every data-center build has to pass through is the interconnection position plus the turbine slot. A data center can buy chips in weeks; it cannot buy an energized grid connection or a 2028 turbine in weeks.

OPINION The rhyme that cuts against us is 1999 to 2001 fiber: real demand, then capacity overbuilt once financing turned. Fiber's bottleneck could be relieved by laying more glass quickly; a turbine slot sold through 2032 and a 5-year median queue cannot. That protects plant owners who already hold grid-connected megawatts more than it protects GEV, which sells the turbines.

OPINION The rhyme that fits GEV specifically is Lucent and Nortel in 2000. GEV does not own the scarce slot the way a grid-connected plant owns its MW. FACT (GEV Q3 release): 58 of the 122 GW are slot reservations, not firm orders. INFERENCE: about 48%. GEV's 10-K warns some counterparties may never place orders equal to their reservation.

1. Who actually has power available

  • FACT: Talen-Amazon covers 1,920 MW at full volume through 2042, ramping to full no later than 2032 (Talen release, 6/11/2025). REPORTED: contract min-max of 240–960 MW in 2026, rising to 1,680–1,920 in 2032.
  • FACT (Talen Q3 call, Nov 3): management cited “480 MW in the 2028 guide” and minimum commits that “ramp to 840 in 2029,” and said the ramp could beat schedule “perhaps by multiple years.”
  • FACT (New Era release, 9/21/2026): Vistra supplies 200–207 MW from its Odessa gas plant for 20 years, with power expected in Q3 2027.
  • FACT (LBNL Queued Up): 2,061 GW sat in US queues at year-end 2025. The median time from request to commercial operation is more than 5 years, and only 13% of 2000–2020 requests made it.
  • FACT (Bloom Q3 release): revenue up 138%, FY26 guidance $4.4–4.6B. REPORTED (call): Oracle's Utah site got power in under 2 months. On-site power is the way around the queue.

2. Pricing power: capacity auction vs turbine slot

  • FACT (PJM 2027/28 BRA report; PJM 2028/29 release): capacity cleared at $269.92/MW-day for 2025/26, then at the cap for three straight auctions: $329.17, $333.44 and $325.00. The last two fell short of the reliability requirement by 6,517 MW and 6,831 MW, and only 774 MW and 525 MW of new generation cleared. ($/MW-day = what a plant is paid per megawatt of capacity per day.)
  • INFERENCE: $325/MW-day is $118,625 per MW-year, about $13.5/MWh on a flat load.
  • OPINION: the cap is setting the price, not the scarcity. Existing plants get paid a capped rate; the turbine slot is where pricing is still free to rise.
  • FACT (GEV Q3 release): 122 GW of gas equipment under contract (64 GW firm plus 58 GW of slot reservations), guided to at least 130 GW by year-end. REPORTED (call): gas pricing per kW is about 50% above FY25, about 20% of customers are data centers, and 2032 delivery slots have been sold.

Upside vs downside

  • Upside (OPINION): PJM falls short again on Dec 22, GEV keeps adding slot reservations, and data-center gas pricing keeps rising.
  • Downside (OPINION): financing tightens, hyperscalers slow data-center spending, slot reservations stop turning into orders, and turbine pricing rolls over. GEV spot at the quote time was $776.49, down sharply on the day.
  • The paper trade caps the downside at the debit.

What would change our opinion

  • Fri Nov 6, 2026, 9 AM CT: Vistra Q3 (FACT, Vistra IR). Mon Nov 9, 7 AM CT: NRG Q3 (FACT, NRG IR). Mon Nov 9, 8 AM CT: Constellation Q3 (REPORTED, not yet confirmed by the company). We're watching for energized MW and new data-center contracts.
  • About Mon Nov 16, 2026: PJM's remaining co-location compliance filing (INFERENCE date). Rules that make it easier for plants to serve co-located load faster would ease the bottleneck.
  • Dec 9–15, 2026: PJM 2029/30 capacity auction bidding, results Tue Dec 22 (FACT). This auction runs under the same FERC price collar, so the cap does not lift. Another shortfall at the cap strengthens the existing-plant case; a clear well below the cap weakens it.
  • After expiry. Wed Jan 27, 2027: GEV Q4 results (FACT date, GEV IR). Tests the view, not the trade: slot reservations or orders falling quarter over quarter, or management saying data-center pricing has cooled, would make us wrong.
  • After expiry. Q3 2027: Vistra–New Era power-on. Is energized MW on time? Tests the view, not the trade.
